## How to play

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:5180 in Chrome or Safari and click to play. You spawn on a 20 m roof looking down the main avenue toward the north Wall.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Mouse | Aim and camera |
| Left / right click (hold) | Left / right hook: fires on press, lets go on release |
| Shift (hold) | Reel in on attached cables (spends gas) |
| Space | Gas boost in the air, jump on the ground, push off a wall |
| WASD | Steer in the air, run on the ground |
| R / Esc | Reset to spawn / pause |
| F3 / F4 | Performance overlay / tuning panel |

The crosshair shows whether a hook fired now will catch:
- **Solid teal:** it will catch.
- **Dashed:** aim assist will snap it to a nearby edge.
- **Faded:** out of range, or nothing to catch. The range is 60 m.

Test worlds for trying one thing at a time:
- **`?play=hook-attach`:** flat ground with a wall 40 m ahead.
- **`?play=side-swing`:** a tall tower.
- **`?play=double-hook`:** two buildings with a gap to thread.
- **`?play=ground-clearance`:** a 12 m street.

Watch a scripted run with `?scenario=replay-swing-chain`.

**Values changed from the spec's starting values: none.**
- Every constant in the spec's starting-values table (120 Hz, 60 m range, 150 m/s hooks, 3 degree assist cone, 40 s of gas, 35 m/s soft top speed, 1.60 m Levi, 50 m Wall, about 1.5 km district, 60 to 75 degree FOV) is used as written.
- The spec gave gravity as a range (9.81 to 20). I chose **16 m/s²**: heavy enough that falls feel committed, light enough that a gas burst lifts you.
- The constants the spec left open are first-time values I chose; they're all in the table. Examples: the 30 m/s winder, 18 m/s reel, and the assist strengths.
- One non-constant change after the first capture: impact shake used trauma squared and was invisible (about 0.1 degrees on a 15 m/s wall hit), so it now scales with trauma to the power 1.5.

## Performance

The official run is `npm run perf`: a 60-second scripted flight at rooftop height over the dense south-east core, with hooks every 2.5 s. It ran on this MacBook Air M4 (16 GB) in Google Chrome on the Apple GPU (`apple metal-3`, not a fallback), at 1470 x 956 points at 2x, with vsync and the frame-rate limit off so the average shows real headroom. Raw numbers are in [perf.json](../../evidence/perf/perf.json).

| Measure | Target | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Average | 60 fps or better | **261.6 fps** |
| 1% low | 45 fps or better | **96.9 fps** |
| Worst frame after control | 100 ms or less | **38.7 ms** (two frames over 25 ms) |
| Render scale | never below 0.75 | **1.0 throughout** |
| Camera inside geometry | never | **0 frames** |

- GPU time was 8.3 ms per frame: 3.2 ms scene, 5.0 ms shadow.
- JavaScript averaged 3.1 ms per frame.
- The system load average was 13 at the end of the run; another session was still active on the laptop.

A reference run with vsync on, which is how you'll play, gave these results ([perf-vsync.json](../../evidence/perf/perf-vsync.json)):
- a locked 60 fps
- 1% low 53.2 fps
- worst frame 18.8 ms, with no frames over 25 ms
- about 3 ms of GPU per 16.7 ms frame

Earlier runs while the laptop was saturated by the other session (load average 40 to 78) produced stalls of up to 120 ms. That is the headroom risk to watch once art arrives in Milestone 3.

## The three weakest things about the feel

1. **In a low town, swinging is mostly zipping.**
   - The facades are only 13 to 20 m tall and sit to the side. So a held hook doesn't carry Levi forward in a big arc; it curls him around the anchor into that facade.
   - The scripted route only flows when it reels toward each anchor (Shift) and lets go before arriving, so it zips from point to point.
   - Real pendulum swings exist off the towers, the Wall and the far side of wide squares, and hardly anywhere else.
   - This may be exactly right for Attack on Titan, or it may feel like the gear isn't swinging. It's the most important thing to judge.
2. **Hooking something ahead doesn't pull you.**
   - While you fly toward an anchor, the cable is slack; the winder just takes up the slack. You keep falling until you reel or pass beneath it.
   - It's physically honest but can feel unresponsive.
   - An alternative is a light automatic pull whenever a hook is attached (the Tribute Game does roughly this). That's a feel change, so I haven't made it.
3. **The ground-clearance assist can yank.**
   - When a swing would sag into the street, the assist shortens the cable at up to 30 m/s. That keeps you off the cobbles but can pop you up onto a roof edge; the 12 m street fixture ends on a roof.
   - Related: the camera lean is only about 1 degree at the default 7-degree setting, because anchors are mostly overhead.
   - Wall stick triggers on most facade hits above 8 m/s, which may feel sticky.

## Things to try

1. From the spawn, look down the avenue and hold left click on the left facade about 30 m ahead, then Shift. Let go of both just before the anchor and fire right on the other side. Does zipping feel good, or do you want more swing?
2. `?play=double-hook`: hook both buildings, reel, and let go in the gap. Does threading feel precise?
3. Hook something far ahead without reeling and feel the slack approach (weak point 2). Would a light automatic pull be better?
4. Swing low down a street (`?play=ground-clearance`) and see whether the clearance assist feels like help or a yank. In F4, set "Ground clearance" to 0 and compare.
5. Run off a roof, fall 20 m and land: stumble at low speed, roll at speed. Is the recovery time right?
6. Slam into a wall at speed, hold, then Space to push off. Is it too sticky? Try "Impact speed to stick" at 12.
7. Reel up the Wall (it's 50 m; hook near the top) and drop onto the land outside.
8. In F4, try gravity 13 and 19, hook speed 250, and assist cone 0 versus 6. Export what you like.
9. Hold Space in the air until the gas runs out. Is 40 s the right amount, and is running dry readable?

## Known limits

- **It's a greybox, as intended.** Flat grid materials, a jointed mannequin, sphere gas puffs, no Titans, and no gas supply points yet.
- **Other work on this laptop hurts frame times.** While another session was compiling Rust and running Pieced (load average up to 78 on 10 cores), a 60-second flight showed stalls of up to 120 ms. The performance section reports the run and the conditions.
- **The perf flight doesn't use real physics.** It flies with zero gravity and endless gas so it can hold rooftop height looking at the horizon, which is the heaviest view. Rendering is the real game; only the pilot's physics is simplified.
